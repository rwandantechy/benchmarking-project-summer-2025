## q1
**Prompt:**

```
Solve the quadratic equation x^2 - 7x + 12 = 0. List all real or complex roots.
```

**Model Answer:**

```
To solve the quadratic equation $x^2 - 7x + 12 = 0$, we can use the quadratic formula, which is given by:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

In this case, $a=1$, $b=-7$, and $c=12$. Plugging these values into the quadratic formula, we get:

$$x = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{(-7)^2 - 4(1)(12)}}{2*1} = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{49 - 48}}{2} = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{1}}{2}$$

Since the square root of 1 is 1, we have two solutions:

$$x_1 = \frac{7 + 1}{2} = 5$$
$$x_2 = \frac{7 - 1}{2} = 3.5$$

Therefore, the real roots of the given quadratic equation are $x=3.5$ and $x=5$. There are no complex roots for this equation since the discriminant $(-7)^2 - 4(1)(12) = 1 > 0$, meaning that the square root in the quadratic formula is real.
```

**Correct:** True

$x=\frac{6\pm\sqrt{(6)^2-(4*1*9)}}{2*1}$

## q4
**Prompt:**

```
Solve the quadratic equation x^2 - 2 = 0. List all real or complex roots.
```

**Model Answer:**

```
To solve the quadratic equation $x^2 - 2 = 0$, we first set it equal to zero:

$x^2 - 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 = 2$.

Now, we take the square root of both sides (remembering that for a real number $b$, the two square roots of $b$ are $\pm\sqrt{b}$). We find:

$x = \pm\sqrt{2}$.

So there are two solutions to this equation: one positive and one negative. In other words, the roots of the quadratic equation are $\sqrt{2}$ and $-\sqrt{2}$. These are real numbers, since they are not complex (i.e., not in the form $a + bi$ for real $a$ and $b$ and non-zero imaginary unit $i$).
```

**Correct:** False
