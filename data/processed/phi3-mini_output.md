Supervised and unsupervised are two primary categories in Machine Learning which differ based on how they learn patterns from data to make predictions or decisions without explicit instructions. Here is a detailed comparison:

1) Input Data Structure: In Supervised learning, the input consists of labeled examples with known outputs (i.incorrect_example). Contrastingly in Unsupervised Learning there's no provided output and it tries to learn patterns from unlabeled data(i.unlabelled_data).
 
2) Model Purpose: Supervised learning aims at predicting the outcome of unknown inputs based on known outputs, essentially mapping input-output pairs (i.input_mapping). Unsupervised Learning focuses more towards identifying patterns or structures in unlabeled data without direct reference to outcomes(i.pattern_identification), sometimes with no specific goal beyond this discovery process like clustering similar examples together for market segmentation analysis, anomaly detection etc.
 
3) Types of Problems: Supervised learning is typically used where the output variable needs prediction (e.g., Predicting House Prices based on features). Conversely Unsupervised Learning finds patterns when outcomes are less clear or irrelevant(i.find_patterns), for instance, segmenting customers into different groups within a target market with similar characteristics but without predefined labels to predict behavior from the group membership alone (clustering problem in unsupervised learning).
 
Example of Supervised Learning: A common example would be using historical sales data(i.historical_sales) and corresponding features like price, color, brand etc.(i.features), along with their outcomes or selling prices to train a model that predicts future product pricing based on input characteristics – this is known as regression problem in supervised learning context.
 
Example of Unsupervised Learning: A typical example would be grouping customers into clusters(i.customer_segmentation) without prior knowledge about their preferences or behaviors, but by analyzing available customer data like demographics and purchase history to identify distinct groups with similar characteristics for targeted marketing strategies – this is often done through techniques such as k-means clustering in unsupervised learning context.
 
In summary: The key difference lies on whether you have labeled or unlabeled input data, the purpose of mapping that relationship (output prediction vs pattern recognition) and problem types they are typically applied to.(i.difference_summary).
