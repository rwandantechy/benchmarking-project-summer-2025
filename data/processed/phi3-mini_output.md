Supervised Learning: This is a type of machine learning where models are trained using labeled data to predict outcomes on new, unseen instances with high accuracy. The model learns from this input-output pairings during training which allows it make predictions or decisions without being specifically programmed for doing so. A classic example in supervised learning is email spam detection: a system can learn over time what characteristics emails have that are often considered to be "spam" by users, such as certain keywords and formatting styles.
 
Unsupervised Learning: In this type of machine learning, the model learns patterns from unlabeled data without any prior knowledge or supervision on outcomes being a part of training process. The goal is typically discovering hidden structures in input data that might not be immediately apparent to human observers. A common example here would be market basket analysis used by large retailers, where the model identifies products often bought together and then uses this knowledge for product placement or recommendations without being specifically directed on what combinations are likely to occur beforehand.
 
In sum: Supervised learning relies heavily on labeled data with known outcomes during training while unsupervised learning makes sense of patterns in the absence of such information, often identifying hidden structures within datasets that can be valuable for decision making or further exploration by humans.
