Supervised Machine Learning: In this approach, algorithms learn from labeled training data to predict outcomes for unseen situations or inputs by finding patterns in the input-output pairs provided during the training phase. Supervised methods involve a target variable that we want our model to predict and include classification (e.g., spam detection) as well as regression problems (predicting housing prices). A simple example of supervised machine learning is email filtering, where an algorithm learns from labeled emails about their 'spam' or 'non-spam' status during training time, then can predict whether new incoming messages are spam.

Unsupervised Machine Learning: This approach involves finding hidden structure in unlabeled data to understand the relationships within it and find insights that might be useful for solving business problems without any prior knowledge about what patterns could exist before running the analysis. Unsupervised methods include clustering (e.g., market segmentation) where we group customers into segments with similar characteristics based on past purchase behavior, yet these groups are not previously defined or labeled in our data set during training time. A simple example of unsupervised machine learning is customer segmentation using the K-means algorithm to divide a company's customer base into distinct clusters for targeted marketing campaigns with similar buying habits and preferences, even though these specific groups were not previously defined in our data set during training time.
