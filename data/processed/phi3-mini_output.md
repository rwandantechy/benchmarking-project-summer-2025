Supervised Machine Learning is a type of algorithm that learns from labeled data to predict outcomes or classify input data into distinct categories based on past examples provided with their correct results (known as labels). It operates under the guidance of human expertise and can be used for tasks such as classification, regression analysis, forecasting etc. The most common algorithms include Linear Regression, Decision Trees, SVM (Support Vector Machines), K-Nearest Neighbors among others.

Example: A classic example is a spam detection system in email services that uses supervised learning. Here the algorithm learns to recognize and classify emails as either 'spam' or 'not spam', using historical data where emails are already labeled accordingly, based on their content such as presence of certain words (e.g., free), sender’s reputation etc. 

Unsupervised Machine Learning is a type of algorithm that learns and finds patterns from unlabeled input data without any guidance or supervision to understand the underlying structure in the dataset with little human intervention, often used for clustering analysis which groups similar entities together based on certain features into clusters (groups) they have commonalities. Common algorithms include K-Means Clustering, Hierarchical Clustering etc.
 
Example: Customer segmentation is an example where unsupervised learning techniques can be utilized to discover distinct segments or 'clusters' of customers based on their buying habits (like past purchases), demographics and psychographics without any predetermined labels being provided for them. The goal here would be grouping similar customer profiles together into clusters, each representing a unique segment which the business can then target with customized marketing strategies or services to cater specifically to their needs.
